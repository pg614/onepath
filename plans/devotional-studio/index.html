Who it's for

A person of faith in their 40s or 50s who has walked through something hard (caring for a parent, burnout, a midlife crossroads) and found God faithful in it. You write short, honest, topical devotionals and small-group studies for people in that same season, and sell them as print-on-demand books on Amazon and as PDF study kits to churches.

Why it's meaningful

Your hardest season becomes someone else's lifeline. A 40-day devotional for a weary caregiver, or a 6-week study for men at midlife, can reach people at 3 a.m. when no one else is awake. It's ministry that can also sustain itself.

The market case

  • US Bible sales reached about 19 million in 2025, a 21-year high (Publishers Weekly).
  • Religion nonfiction print sales rose 5.4% in 2025 (Publishers Weekly).
  • Amazon caregiver devotionals are priced about $12.98–$19.99 in paperback, for example Grace for the Sandwich Generation at $13.99 and Poured Out at $19.99 (Amazon, Amazon); IVP's Hope for Caregivers is $16.81 (Amazon).
  • Etsy printable devotionals and Bible-study PDFs mostly sell for about $1.75–$9.99 (Etsy, Etsy).
  • Lifeway small-group leader kits run about $37.99–$40.99 per group (Lifeway). Prices seen Nov 2026.

Your angle: the shelf is crowded with generic devotionals. Win with a tight topic (men at midlife, burned-out ministry leaders, sandwich-generation caregivers), a real personal story, and a matching small-group kit churches can use right away.

How AI does the heavy lifting

  • Helps you outline 40 days around one theme and turns your voice memos into clean first drafts. The heart and the testimony stay yours.
  • Checks every Scripture reference and flags any translation-permission issues.
  • Formats the interior for KDP, drafts cover concepts and writes your Amazon description and keywords.
  • Builds the small-group kit: leader guide, discussion questions, slides and handouts.
  • Drafts outreach to pastors and small-group directors for you to review and send.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1: Choose ONE audience and theme. AI researches the top 20 competing books. Record your story and outline 40 days.
  2. Week 2: Write 10 devotionals and share them with 5 people in that season for honest feedback.
  3. Week 3: Finish the draft, format it, order a proof copy, and build the 6-week study from the same themes.
  4. Week 4: Publish on KDP and Etsy, then offer the study free to 2 local churches in exchange for feedback and a testimonial.

Finding your first customers

  • Your own church and your pastor's network: offer a free pilot group.
  • Small-group directors, men's and women's ministry leaders, and caregiver support groups at churches.
  • Christian Facebook groups for your audience: share one free devotional where allowed.
  • Amazon: strong keywords, a clear subtitle and a few honest early reviews from real readers (never paid or swapped).

Risks and honest cautions

  • Crowded shelf. Without a sharp topic and audience, books get buried.
  • Amazon ranking takes time and reviews; ads can lose money if you rush them.
  • Theology matters. Ask a pastor or trusted mentor to read it before you publish.
  • Respect copyright: keep Scripture quotes within each translation's permission limits.
  • AI drafts must be rewritten in your voice. Readers can tell, and Amazon asks you to disclose AI-generated content.

The honest verdict

Reasons to build

  • Readers are buying: Bible and religion nonfiction sales are up (Publishers Weekly).
  • Almost no startup cost and no inventory with print-on-demand.
  • Church kits turn one book into a group product.

Reasons to pause

  • The shelf is crowded. A vague topic gets buried.
  • Amazon ranking takes time and honest reviews.
  • Theology and Scripture copyright need careful checking.

- My story (why this matters to me):
- Hours per week I can give:
- Startup budget I'm comfortable with: $
- Faith/values I want reflected (optional):
- Tools I already have (website, email list, social accounts):

MISSION
Create and sell short topical devotionals and small-group Bible studies for people in a specific hard season (for example caregivers, burnout, men at midlife): print-on-demand paperbacks and ebooks on Amazon KDP, printable PDFs on Etsy and my own site, and small-group kits for churches. Keep my voice and testimony at the heart. Check every Scripture reference and respect translation copyright limits.

GROUND RULES (never break these)
1. Never post, publish, email, message, buy, sign up for anything or spend money without showing me the exact draft and getting my clear "yes" first.
2. Never make income or earnings promises in any copy. Promise clarity and help, not money.
3. Never invent numbers. Cite a source for every market figure, or mark it "unverified."
4. Give me ONE clear next step at a time, in plain words. If I seem overwhelmed, shrink the step.
5. Keep a running "Decisions & Progress" log and show it at every check-in.

BUILD IN THIS ORDER. Finish each phase and get my okay before starting the next.
Phase 1, Research: Find 10 real competitors with their prices and links. Find where my customers gather online and offline and what they complain about. Summarize the gap I can fill in 5 bullets. Ask me 5 questions to sharpen my angle.
Phase 2, Offer: Draft my core offer, a starter offer and a price ladder, with cost and margin per sale. Write the promise in one sentence with no income claims.
Phase 3, Site: Draft a simple one-page website (headline, who it's for, how it works, offer, about me, FAQ, signup). Recommend the cheapest platform. Hand me the copy and a setup checklist; don't publish until I approve.
Phase 4, Content: Create a 4-week content calendar (2 helpful posts a week) plus one free lead magnet. Draft the first 4 posts for my review.
Phase 5, Customer outreach: Build a list of 25 places and people to reach (groups, partners, referral sources). Draft personal, non-spammy messages. I send them myself, or you send only after my okay on each one.
Phase 6, Products: Outline and draft a 40-day devotional from my story and voice memos (I rewrite and approve every day), then the matching 6-week small-group kit. Prepare KDP formatting, cover briefs, Amazon description and keywords, and the Etsy listing, all for my approval before anything is published.
Phase 7, Weekly routines: Set up a weekly routine. Every Monday: show me last week's numbers (visitors, signups, conversations, sales), what worked, and the ONE most important task for this week. Every Thursday: a 5-minute check-in to see if I'm stuck.
If you can create helper bots or routines, create them in this order: Research bot, Site bot, Content bot, Outreach bot, Weekly Check-in routine. Each must follow the same ground rules.

Start now with Phase 1. First, read my details above and ask me anything missing.
