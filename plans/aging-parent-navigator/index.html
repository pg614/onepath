The Aging Parent Navigator

A calm guide for adult children in the first 90 days of caring for a parent.

Who it's for

Someone in their 40s or 50s who has already walked the hard road of caring for a parent: the hospital discharge, the Medicare maze, the "who has power of attorney?" talk, the move to assisted living. You learned it the hard way. Now you guide other adult children through their first 90 days so they don't have to.

Important: you are a guide and organizer, not a licensed care manager, nurse, lawyer or financial advisor. You help people get organized, ask the right questions and find the right professionals.

Why it's meaningful

The first weeks after a parent's fall or diagnosis are some of the most frightening of a person's life. A calm voice who has been there, with a checklist and a plan, turns panic into next steps. This is work that honors your own parent's story.

The market case

  • 63 million Americans are family caregivers, up 45% since 2015 (AARP 2025).
  • Only about 11% say they've had training for the tasks they do (AARP 2025).
  • Professional geriatric care managers typically charge $150–$300 an hour (AARP), out of reach for many families. That's the gap: a lower-cost, do-it-with-you guide.
  • Competitors: Harvard Medical School's self-paced Caregiver's Guide course is $29.95 (HMS); ElderHonor's Complete Caregiving Toolkit is $197 (ElderHonor); printable caregiver binders on Etsy run about $13–$20 (Etsy). Prices seen Nov 2026.

Your angle: none of these give a personal, first-90-days path with a real person who's been there. That's what you sell.

Break-even example

Example math, not a prediction of earnings. If your monthly costs were about $40 (tools plus a share of your AI plan), selling one $49 toolkit a month would cover them. Covering a $200 month would take, for example, one planning call plus one basic toolkit. Many new sellers make zero sales in their first months.

How AI does the heavy lifting

  • Turns your real experience into polished checklists, scripts and a 40-page binder in days, not months.
  • Researches your state's Medicaid, Area Agency on Aging and elder-law resources so your toolkit links to real local help (you double-check them).
  • Writes your website, emails and helpful posts in your voice.
  • Drafts each call summary from your notes so a 60-minute call doesn't take 3 hours.
  • Runs a weekly check-in so you keep moving.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1: Write your own story and the 10 things you wish you'd known. AI turns it into the toolkit outline. Research local resources.
  2. Week 2: Build the $49 toolkit and a one-page site with a free “First 7 Days” checklist to collect emails.
  3. Week 3: Offer 3 free planning calls to friends-of-friends in exchange for honest feedback and a testimonial (with permission).
  4. Week 4: Fix what they found confusing, open sales, and post helpful answers in 3 caregiver groups.

Finding your first customers

  • Caregiver Facebook groups and Reddit's r/AgingParents: answer questions generously; share your free checklist only where rules allow.
  • Churches, senior centers and hospital discharge planners: offer a free talk, “Your parent's first 90 days.”
  • Elder-law attorneys, financial planners and senior-move managers: they meet panicked families every week and want someone to refer them to.
  • HR teams at local employers: caregiving is a top reason people miss work.

Risks and honest cautions

  • Stay in your lane. Never give medical, legal or financial advice. Say “ask your doctor, elder-law attorney or advisor,” and put that in writing on every product.
  • Emotionally heavy work. Set limits on calls and care for yourself.
  • Families in crisis are price-sensitive. Keep a free resource and a low-cost entry option.
  • Local rules vary. Check whether your state regulates “care management” titles before you use them.
  • Results take time. Trust is built slowly in this space.

GROUND RULES (never break these)
1. Never post, publish, email, message, buy, sign up for anything or spend money without showing me the exact draft and getting my clear "yes" first.
2. Never make income or earnings promises in any copy. Promise clarity and help, not money.
3. Never invent numbers. Cite a source for every market figure, or mark it "unverified."
4. Give me ONE clear next step at a time, in plain words. If I seem overwhelmed, shrink the step.
5. Keep a running "Decisions & Progress" log and show it at every check-in.

BUILD IN THIS ORDER. Finish each phase and get my okay before starting the next.
Phase 1, Research: Find 10 real competitors with their prices and links. Find where my customers gather online and offline and what they complain about. Summarize the gap I can fill in 5 bullets. Ask me 5 questions to sharpen my angle.
Phase 2, Offer: Draft my core offer, a starter offer and a price ladder, with cost and margin per sale. Write the promise in one sentence with no income claims.
Phase 3, Site: Draft a simple one-page website (headline, who it's for, how it works, offer, about me, FAQ, signup). Recommend the cheapest platform. Hand me the copy and a setup checklist; don't publish until I approve.
Phase 4, Content: Create a 4-week content calendar (2 helpful posts a week) plus one free lead magnet. Draft the first 4 posts for my review.
Phase 5, Customer outreach: Build a list of 25 places and people to reach (groups, partners, referral sources). Draft personal, non-spammy messages. I send them myself, or you send only after my okay on each one.
Phase 6, Products: Turn my story into the First 90 Days Toolkit (checklists, conversation scripts, document binder, local resource list for my state, verified with links). Draft the planning-call intake form and a call-summary template.
Phase 7, Weekly routines: Set up a weekly routine. Every Monday: show me last week's numbers (visitors, signups, conversations, sales), what worked, and the ONE most important task for this week. Every Thursday: a 5-minute check-in to see if I'm stuck.
If you can create helper bots or routines, create them in this order: Research bot, Site bot, Content bot, Outreach bot, Weekly Check-in routine. Each must follow the same ground rules.
