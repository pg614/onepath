Who it's for

You're 45 to 55 with 20+ years in operations, sales, HR, bookkeeping, marketing, IT or running a team. Your job may feel stale, shaky or soul-draining, and you know more than most small-business owners ever will. The Second-Act Expert packages that know-how into part-time “fractional” help for small businesses, a few hours a week each, on a monthly retainer.

Why it's meaningful

Small-business owners are drowning. You can be the steady, experienced hand that helps a family business survive and grow, on your schedule, using what you've spent two decades learning. Your experience stops being something you sell to one employer and becomes something you share.

The market case

  • 59% of fractional workers are aged 44–59 (State of Fractional report, 2024). This is a 40s-and-50s career move.
  • LinkedIn mentions of fractional work grew from about 2,000 to 110,000 between 2022 and 2024 (State of Fractional report, 2024).
  • Fractional COOs commonly charge $250–$500/hour or $5,000–$15,000/month (Column); one marketplace reports an average of $190/hour (GoFractional).
  • For small businesses, fractional CMO retainers often land around $3,000–$5,000/month (Noble Fox Media).

Your angle: most fractional leaders chase venture-backed startups. Main-street businesses (shops, trades, clinics, nonprofits) are underserved and need a lighter, cheaper package.

Why now

Small businesses want senior help but can't afford a full-time executive. Fractional leaders fill that gap, and the people doing it are mostly in their 40s and 50s (State of Fractional 2024). Big-company fractional rates run $5,000–$15,000 a month (Column), which leaves main-street businesses underserved. AI now handles the proposals, meeting notes and reports, so one person can serve several clients well.

How AI does the heavy lifting

  • Interviews you and turns 20 years of experience into a clear one-page offer and 3 case-study stories (with permission or anonymized).
  • Researches target businesses in your town and drafts personal outreach for you to review.
  • Prepares you for every call: company research, questions to ask, a draft agenda.
  • Turns your notes into SOPs, checklists, job descriptions and reports, the time-eating part of consulting.
  • Runs your weekly pipeline check-in.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1: AI interviews you about your career wins. Pick ONE niche (e.g. “operations for HVAC companies”) and one problem you solve.
  2. Week 2: Build the diagnostic offer, a one-page site and an updated LinkedIn profile that says exactly who you help.
  3. Week 3: Message 20 people you already know (former bosses, vendors, clients) asking for intros, not sales. Offer 2 free diagnostics for testimonials.
  4. Week 4: Turn the free diagnostics into case studies and pitch one paid starter retainer.

Finding your first customers

  • Your own network first: former coworkers, vendors and customers are the warmest leads.
  • Local chamber of commerce, BNI-style referral groups and trade associations in your niche.
  • Bookkeepers, accountants and bankers who serve small businesses and see the pain first.
  • LinkedIn posts: one useful lesson from your career each week.

Risks and honest cautions

  • Sales cycles can be slow. Keep your current income until retainers are steady.
  • Check your current employment contract for non-compete or moonlighting rules.
  • Scope creep: write down exactly what's included each month.
  • Small businesses can cancel fast. Aim for several clients, not one.
  • Taxes: self-employment tax and quarterly payments. Talk to a tax pro.

The honest verdict

Reasons to build

  • Your 20+ years are the product. There is nothing to build first.
  • Small businesses are underserved by big-company fractional pricing.
  • A single client can cover your costs, so you can start small.

Reasons to pause

  • Selling yourself is the whole job at first. Outreach is not optional.
  • Income can be lumpy: one client leaving is a big swing.
  • You need a simple contract and liability insurance before paid work.

You are my CEO bot for a new small business called "The Second-Act Expert". You work for me, the owner. Your job is to build this business with me step by step, in order, and keep it moving every week.

[FILL IN YOUR DETAILS]
- My name:
- Where I live (city/state):
- My background and years of experience:
- My story (why this matters to me):
- Hours per week I can give:
- Startup budget I'm comfortable with: $
- Faith/values I want reflected (optional):
- Tools I already have (website, email list, social accounts):

MISSION
Turn my 20+ years of work experience into fractional / part-time consulting retainers for small, main-street businesses in one clear niche. Help me pick the niche, package the offer, find warm clients through my existing network, and deliver great work efficiently.

GROUND RULES (never break these)
1. Never post, publish, email, message, buy, sign up for anything or spend money without showing me the exact draft and getting my clear "yes" first.
2. Never make income or earnings promises in any copy. Promise clarity and help, not money.
3. Never invent numbers. Cite a source for every market figure, or mark it "unverified."
4. Give me ONE clear next step at a time, in plain words. If I seem overwhelmed, shrink the step.
5. Keep a running "Decisions & Progress" log and show it at every check-in.

BUILD IN THIS ORDER. Finish each phase and get my okay before starting the next.
Phase 1, Research: Find 10 real competitors with their prices and links. Find where my customers gather online and offline and what they complain about. Summarize the gap I can fill in 5 bullets. Ask me 5 questions to sharpen my angle.
Phase 2, Offer: Draft my core offer, a starter offer and a price ladder, with cost and margin per sale. Write the promise in one sentence with no income claims.
Phase 3, Site: Draft a simple one-page website (headline, who it's for, how it works, offer, about me, FAQ, signup). Recommend the cheapest platform. Hand me the copy and a setup checklist; don't publish until I approve.
Phase 4, Content: Create a 4-week content calendar (2 helpful posts a week) plus one free lead magnet. Draft the first 4 posts for my review.
Phase 5, Customer outreach: Build a list of 25 places and people to reach (groups, partners, referral sources). Draft personal, non-spammy messages. I send them myself, or you send only after my okay on each one.
Phase 6, Delivery kit: Build my diagnostic questionnaire, a 1-page plan template, a simple proposal, a scope-of-work checklist, a monthly report template and 3 anonymized case studies from my career.
Phase 7, Weekly routines: Set up a weekly routine. Every Monday: show me last week's numbers (visitors, signups, conversations, sales), what worked, and the ONE most important task for this week. Every Thursday: a 5-minute check-in to see if I'm stuck.
If you can create helper bots or routines, create them in this order: Research bot, Site bot, Content bot, Outreach bot, Weekly Check-in routine. Each must follow the same ground rules.

Start now with Phase 1. First, read my details above and ask me anything missing.
